# O-Desk Exchange

## 0.2.0

The twelve names on the board stop pretending to be a real exchange: Vapourware Systems, Wheely Fast
Motors, Petri & Sons, Beach Sand Semis, Ship Happens Freight, Big Wind Energy, Clanky Robotics,
Piggy Bank Holdings, Impulse Buy Retail, One More Turn Games, Rock Bottom Mining and Squint Optics,
with a wire to match: a regulator that brings snacks, insiders trimming for entirely personal
reasons, a founder discovering social media.

**Breaking news** drops in over the board when a story lands, carrying the ticker, the move it is
worth and the headline. It goes after eight seconds, an × closes it sooner, and touching it opens
that name's chart. The first story seen after a reload is swallowed, since a wire that has been
running all along is history rather than news. The popup has its own switch in the settings and in
the companion app.

A headline that named its company twice printed the placeholder the second time. It replaces every
one of them now rather than the first.

## 0.1.3

The app is called **O-Desk Exchange** now, on the store card, on the device and in the tab it opens
in. Nothing else about it changes: same id, same settings, same data, so an installed copy updates
in place and simply reads O-Desk Exchange afterwards.

## 0.1.2

An icon of its own for the store and the launcher.

## 0.1.1

The clock in the header now comes from the daemon's zone rather than the browser's, which reports
UTC on the kiosk whatever the device's own clock says.

## 0.1.0

A fictional stock exchange for the dashboard: twelve invented companies, a live board with
sparklines, a chart at four spans, a headline wire whose stories actually move the prices they are
about, and a ticker tape along the bottom. Prices are a pure function of the clock, so the market
has a history you can chart at any span and a reload never loses it. Settings on the device cover
pace, volatility, colours, chart span, the watchlist and holding the market still.

Nothing here is a real quote.
